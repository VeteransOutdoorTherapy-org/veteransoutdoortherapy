# Writing copy for this site

This section governs every word a visitor reads: page copy, headings, event and field-story text, captions, alt text, meta descriptions, button labels, and the seed and database values behind them. Write the way the organization sees things, not the way a generic nonprofit site sounds.

## What we believe

Healing is not a one-time event. It deepens as a person builds skills, takes on new challenges, reconnects with community, and comes back to help others. The organization does not offer a one-time fix; it offers a pathway: learn safely, practice confidently, take on meaningful challenges, and return to lead.

The trips are about camaraderie and what time in open country gives people: hard effort in fresh air, quiet when quiet is needed, and the company of people who understand without being told. Healing does not end when a trip does. It grows when people who understand service and sacrifice come back to help others find hope, purpose, and community.

## The mission (canonical)

Quote verbatim wherever a full mission statement belongs. Derive shorter versions from it; do not invent new framing.

> Here at Veteran's Outdoor Therapy, we strive to provide fully paid for Outdoor Adventures for America's heroes in a way to honor the service of Soldiers who have faced combat and, in some cases, incurred the wounds of War (We also serve gold star families and children). These outdoor experiences offer a unique combination of physical activity, camaraderie, and emotional healing, allowing participants to reconnect with nature and themselves. Engaging in activities such as hiking, fishing, hunting, and camping not only serves as a refreshing escape from daily life but also creates opportunities for reflection and bonding with fellow veterans who share similar experiences and challenges. These adventures can greatly reduce stress, enhance mental well-being, and cultivate a sense of community and resilience, ultimately aiding in the transition to civilian life. By focusing on the well-being of our nation's heroes, we recognize their sacrifices and contribute positively to their healing and personal growth journey.

The mission's "fully paid for" and "Soldiers who have faced combat" are mission language. They do not license "fully funded" in applicant copy, and they do not narrow eligibility (see below). Ask before carrying either phrase somewhere new.

## Core narrative (Crawl-Walk-Run-Lead)

From the organization's Crawl-Walk-Run-Lead Framework, with names and terms corrected to the site's conventions. It is the source for grant and donor copy; public pages take its ideas, not its wording.

> Veteran's Outdoor Therapy provides transformative outdoor experiences that help Veterans and Gold Star family members reconnect with purpose, community, and identity. Through horseback riding, fishing, hunting, hiking, archery, and peer-led mentorship, participants are given opportunities not only to heal, but to become future volunteers, mentors, and leaders who support others facing similar challenges. Using a Crawl-Walk-Run-Lead model, VOT turns individual outdoor experiences into a progressive pathway of learning, confidence, challenge, service, and belonging.

The four stages, and what each one is about:

| Stage | Word | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crawl | Learn | A safe introduction: foundations, gear, basic skills, and an achievable first success. Comfort and trust. |
| Walk | Practice | Guided practice, growing independence, confidence built through repetition and shared experience. |
| Run | Challenge | A demanding experience — an extended trail ride, a pack-in camp, a multi-day fishing trip, a hunt. Voluntary and supported. |
| Lead | Serve | Coming back as a volunteer, peer mentor, instructor, event support, or mission leader. |

The lasting outcome is belonging and purpose. The pathways run through horseback riding and horsemanship, archery and hunting, fishing and water, and hiking and outdoor adventure.

**How the framework shapes copy.**

- Write about a person's time with the organization as a path that deepens, not a single event they attend. "Our hope is that it outlasts the trip" is the right instinct.
- Progression is an opportunity, never a requirement. A person may repeat a stage, pause, switch activities, or stay involved off the field. Never make Lead sound like a debt owed or a condition of being served.
- The strongest story the organization has is someone who came to be served and returned to serve — the horseback rider who went from Veteran in need, to being served, to serving other Veterans; the angler who volunteered for the next trip so another Veteran could go. Tell those stories when the records support them.
- Quality over quantity. Small groups make room for trust, real instruction, and conversation. Measure impact by depth — people who return, skills gained, people who come back to mentor — not by headcount. Do not boast attendance numbers.
- The framework is a strategic concept draft written for program development, grants, partners, and donors. Do not label events as Crawl, Walk, or Run, or advertise mentor or instructor roles as open, unless Matt says that program is live.

## Where the framework's language belongs

The framework carries grant and program-management language: safety first, qualified instruction, risk planning, contact hours, milestones, after-action reviews, measurement. That language belongs in grant narratives, partner and sponsor pages where a funder is deciding, and factual FAQ answers where someone has asked. It does not belong in headings, program pages, or application pages, where it reads as disclaimers. Safety is how the organization operates; it is not the pitch.

"Transformative" and "equine" are grant words. On the site, prefer the concrete: what happened, where, and what people carried home. Say "horseback riding" or "horsemanship", not "equine activities".

## Voice

- Camaraderie, the outdoors, the hosts who make trips possible, and what people take away. That is the subject.
- Do not police who qualifies. Eligibility — previously deployed Veterans, and Gold Star family members — is a criterion, stated on application and eligibility pages only, never a value and never repeated in every section.
- Do not pre-empt objections or reassure the reader about things they did not ask. No "you don't have to tell us your war stories" — it raises trauma the reader did not bring up.
- Do not write ethics or conduct disclaimers as selling points (fair chase, regulations, what a participant is responsible for).
- Say true things plainly. Aphorisms that reach for a phrase ("The fire is the meeting") were cut; state what the field notes actually record.
- A Veteran applies, and the team finds where they fit. Never imply someone applies to one named trip.

## Money

- Never tell a participant the organization covers their personal costs. No "no trip fee", "we pay your gas", "we'll get you gear", "selected applicants pay nothing", and no "fully funded" anywhere an applicant reads it.
- Do not overcorrect with statements of what the applicant owes. In applicant copy, say nothing about money and talk about the experience.
- Donor and sponsor copy may say what a gift pays for: tags, licenses, lodging, meals, guides, gear that stays with the program, fuel for the trucks and boats. Describe what the gift makes possible ("holds a place in the field for a Veteran"), not what the participant avoids paying. Never name the cancellation cost publicly.

## No filler

- Add copy only when it is asked for. When a layout leaves a gap, fix the layout; do not fill it with a sentence.
- Never state what the reader can already see or what is self-evident. A link to the calendar needs no line saying there are more events.
- Do not repeat a proper noun from the heading in the text beneath it, or repeat the page's title and summary in its closing band.

## Claims the site cannot back

- Do not invent testimonials, quotes, statistics, outcomes, or participant stories. Use only what the records, field notes, and testimonials already hold.
- Name hosts and sponsors only on trips that have already happened. Naming a host on an upcoming trip commits them to something publicly. Never misname a sponsor or host.
- Alt text for event photos is the general tag, for example "Images from the 2026 Poker Run". Never describe who or what is in the frame. No `title` tooltips on images.

## Names and spelling

- The organization is **Veteran's Outdoor Therapy**, with the apostrophe, everywhere. Source documents often omit it; correct it. In code, use `SITE_NAME`.
- **Veteran** is capitalized in copy.
- A **Gold Star family member** is one person — the spouse, parent, child, or sibling of a service member who died. Address that one person; nobody applies "as a family". "Gold Star families" is fine when describing the population served.
- American spelling in everything a visitor reads: practicing, traveled, license, honor, center, gray, program, toward. Leave the stored status value `"cancelled"` alone; it is data.
- Field-story titles lead with the moment and carry the place ("White River fly fishing", "Flint Hills turkey hunt"), not "Veterans" followed by a verb.
